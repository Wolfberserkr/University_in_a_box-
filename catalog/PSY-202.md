# PSY-202 — Judgment and Decision

**Level:** 200 · **Credits:** 3 · **Weeks:** 14 · **Load:** ~5 h/week
**Prerequisite:** PSY-101 · **Unblocks:** PSY-401 (directly — this is the course the capstone is built out of)

---

## 1. Question

**How does a mind decide, and how does it fail in patterned, predictable directions?**

## 2. Why 200-level

Primary sources by default, and the student must hold two incompatible frameworks at once — heuristics-and-biases versus ecological rationality — without collapsing either into the other. Weeks 8–9 are the primary-source-only block.

## 4. Cut list

| Cut | Reason |
|---|---|
| Prospect theory's formal apparatus | The qualitative result is what the capstone uses. The math unblocks nothing here. |
| Neuroeconomics and decision imaging | Weak evidentiary base, high cost, no downstream week. |
| Nudge / choice architecture as policy | A different question — how to change decisions, not how to audit them. |
| The full behavioral-econ bias catalogue | PSY-101's filter kills most of it on sight. Weeks 3–4 cover the survivors. |

## 5. Sources

Meehl is the highest-value item in the major for your destination, and the hardest to obtain. **Start looking in Term A**, not the week you need it — Dawes, Faust & Meehl 1989 is the accessible substitute if the 1954 monograph can't be had.

**Obtainability:** Kahneman and *Noise* are trade. Tetlock is trade. Meehl 1954 may need a library or a used copy.

## 6. Assessment

- **Midterm wk 7:** you must argue Gigerenzer's position against your own week-3 notes convincingly. A student who thinks the dispute is settled has not understood it.
- **Term paper, 3,000 words — the pre-registered framework.** Not an essay. This is the capstone's scoring instrument, timestamped in the Registrar before PSY-401 opens. `ASSESSMENT.md` C1 makes any later modification a FAIL, so week 14 is load-bearing in a way no other term paper is.
