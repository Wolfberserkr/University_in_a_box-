# PSY-301 — The Social Animal

**Level:** 300 · **Credits:** 3 · **Weeks:** 14 · **Load:** ~5 h/week
**Prerequisite:** PSY-201 or PSY-202 · **Unblocks:** PSY-401

---

## 1. Question

**What does the presence of others do to a person, and how much of what looks like character is situation?**

## 2. Why 300-level

The student reads the **disputes, not the settlements**. Every classic in this course arrives with its autopsy attached, and the assessment demands original analysis on material the field has never seen — your own observations, which is the point of running this major from where you sit.

## 4. Cut list

| Cut | Reason |
|---|---|
| Attitude-change literature (ELM, dual-route) | Large, old, heavily affected by the crisis, unblocks nothing here. |
| Implicit bias measurement and the IAT | The predictive-validity dispute would consume three weeks and settle nothing the capstone needs. |
| Social neuroscience | Same reason as PSY-201's neuroscience cut. |
| Organizational behavior as a field | Week 13 takes what's needed. The rest is a different degree. |

## 5. Sources

Ross & Nisbett and Goffman are the two to acquire early. Goffman is the surprise: written in 1959, about sociology, and more directly useful to a decision audit conducted from a surveillance context than anything else in the major.

**Obtainability:** clear. All trade or library-standard.

## 6. Assessment

- **Midterm wk 7:** you must work a real case from your own week in both directions — as disposition and as situation — and say which reading you'd bet on and why.
- **Term paper, 4,000 words — original analysis:** *"A situation I misread as a character trait."* `ASSESSMENT.md` 300-level scaling binds: the analysis runs on your own material, and it must name what would have shown you were wrong at the time.
