# PSY-302 — Personality and the Individual

**Level:** 300 · **Credits:** 3 · **Weeks:** 14 · **Load:** ~5 h/week
**Prerequisite:** PSY-201 · **Unblocks:** PSY-401

---

## 1. Question

**What is actually stable about a person, and what did we only think was stable?**

## 2. Why 300-level

The most theory-laden material in the major, and the course most likely to be read badly by someone without PSY-101. Weeks 11–12 require holding an influential book and its rigorous counterweight simultaneously without resolving the tension prematurely.

Weeks 11–12 are the course. Separating the strong trauma research from the overreach, with both books open, is the exact skill PSY-401 needs when reading an incident file written by someone with a theory.

## 4. Cut list

| Cut | Reason |
|---|---|
| Freud, Jung, psychoanalysis as content | Historically enormous, empirically inert. One reading as intellectual history, no more. |
| Psychometric construction and factor analysis | You need to read a trait score, not build an instrument. |
| Clinical treatment literature | This is a course about what a person *is*, not how to change one. Different degree. |
| Positive psychology interventions | Among the hardest-hit areas in replication. Not worth remediating. |
| The anti-psychiatry movement as politics | Week 13 takes the methodological critique, which is the strong version. |

## 5. Sources

**Acquire #11 and #12 together or neither.** Reading van der Kolk without McNally is how this course fails, and it fails in the specific direction of making you more confident and less accurate — the same failure mode as deception-detection training in PSY-202 wk 13.

**Obtainability:** clear. Turkheimer 2000 is open and is the highest value-per-page item in the major.

## 6. Assessment

- **Midterm wk 7:** heritability, stated correctly, with one case where an environmental intervention *raised* it. A student who defines heritability as "the proportion caused by genes" has not passed.
- **Term paper, 4,000 words — original analysis:** *"What I can and cannot infer about a person from twenty minutes of watching them."* Written against your own occupational instincts, which is what makes it a 300-level paper rather than a summary.
